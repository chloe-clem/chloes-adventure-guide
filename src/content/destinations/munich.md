Munich is one of the most expensive cities to live in in Germany, and you can definitely feel a "higher end" vibe when walking around the city. I swear everyone is dressed so well. I remember walking around, probably wearing multi-day old clothes in true backpacking fashion, and feeling crusty with a capital C compared to the stunning people that filled the streets.

It's a very enjoyable city, and I've been both in the summer and winter to visit friends. The Nymphenburg Palace Park was a beautiful place to walk, and it was fun to see people ice skating on the frozen water. I also visited the English Garden which is a giant park in Munich, one of the biggest inner-city parks in the world. 

Along the river, you can actually find people surfing, even in the winter! In the summer, I spent a hot day swimming here which was so refreshing and relaxing. I wish I could go back.

One of the coolest (and maybe most dangerous) experiences I had in Munich was standing on a bridge as the clock struck midnight to celebrate New Year's. At the time, I was wrapping up my study abroad in The Netherlands and a couple of us took the trip to Germany to celebrate with our friends that were from there. I remember running through the streets to make it to the top of this bridge in time for the count down. People were shooting fireworks off left and right, out of beer cans and probably anything you can think of. It was pretty magical, thrilling, and terrifying all at the same time.

Not to get too sappy, but it was one of those pinch me moments that I can still remember so clearly. One of those times where you feel an overwhelm of gratitude and pure contentment with the way things are in that very moment, never wanting them to change. Here I was in a new city, surrounded by friends I had only met a couple months ago, building incredible memories with them. We were all huddled together on the top of this bridge, one friend had his news program playing from his phone to moderate our countdown and signal when to start shoving green grapes down our mouths. I remember laughing so hard, I could barely get them down! 

It's these exact moments that make me love travel so much and appreciate the good that comes out of expanding your bubble and connecting with people from all over the world. I am so grateful for the memories I've made in Munich.
