I've been to Cologne twice. Once as a quick pit stop while waiting on a train and the other to visit a friend who lives there! Both very different experiences.

The first time I really stayed close to the train station. Of course I saw the cathedral, just right outside the station, which was incredible both inside and out. I spent hours walking through it and enjoying the stillness inside. It's amazing how such an enormous structure was built in 1248! 

I also toured the Lindt chocolate factory (I know, very random) and this was definitely a highlight because if you know me, you know I have an unhealthy obsession with chocolate. I would say the actual tour is less important, but I recommend checking out the store and restaurant if you're looking to kill some time. 

I also enjoyed just walking by the river. It definitely brings some life to the city and is a great place to hang out or grab a meal on a sunny day.

Besides these two things, I can't say I really fell in love with Cologne. I thought it seemed a bit iffy by the train station, and I really didn't see much of the city or experience the people.

Going back to visit my friend totally changed my opinion, and I got to see a whole other side to the city. I started to see Cologne less as a random city I visited with a church and chocolate factory and instead as an incredibly inviting place to live. It feels like the type of city that welcomes everyone and is filled with lots to do and ways to get involved. It carries a very international, accepting, and relaxed culture. Visually, the city seems more modern with less of the historic charm I've seen in other cities, but I can see why it's a great place to live because of the people and the culture. 

I got to walk around the university, bike around the city, explore the botanical garden, and even attend my friend's frisbee golf tournament! Everyone I met was so inviting!
